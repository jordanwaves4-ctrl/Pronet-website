Questions funders actually ask.

Most of what a partner needs to know before committing is documentary: who selects the community, what the money buys, and what happens when a pump breaks in year three.

Funding & partnership

Can we fund a specific community?

Yes. Send us the district or the criteria and we will come back with two or three assessed communities that fit, with household numbers, existing water points, drilling conditions and a costed budget for each. You choose which one proceeds.

How is a project budget broken down?

Four programme lines and one support line: water (drilling, pump, or mechanisation), sanitation (latrine slabs and mason labour), hygiene (materials, officers' time, follow-up visits), training (committees, caretakers, spares box), and administration capped at 12%.

Monitoring at three, twelve and sixty months is costed into the water line from the start rather than added later.

Do you build directly, or do we appoint the contractor?

We build directly with our own technical department, which is usually the reason a local organisation can be cheaper and faster than an international contractor. If your procurement rules require your own contractor, we will supervise the works, run the community and hygiene components, and hand over the full monitoring record.

What do you need from us before you can quote?

The region or district, the number of communities or people, the outcome you must report on, and any deadline. From that we run the assessment and produce a costed proposal — typically within three weeks.

What does reporting look like?

Monthly narrative and photographic progress, quarterly financials against the agreed line items, and a completion report with functionality data. After handover the site enters our monitoring cycle: three months, twelve months, then annually to year five.

How much goes to overheads?

Administration is capped at 12% of any project budget. Salaries of technical and social staff are charged to the programme lines they work on, not to administration.

Are you registered and audited?

ProNet is registered in Ghana and audited annually. Registration documents, the latest audited accounts and three years of project files are available as a capability pack on request.

Can we visit a site before funding?

Please do. We can arrange a visit to a completed site and an assessed community within a fortnight, including meetings with the water committee and the district authority.

How we work

How are communities selected?

A community approaches us through its assembly, or a district authority refers it. We then verify need — distance to the nearest safe point, failure history, water quality — and check that the community will contribute labour and form a committee. Communities that will not are not selected.

What happens after handover?

The pump belongs to the community, held in trust by the water committee, with a caretaker trained on routine maintenance and a spares box paid for out of a small water fee. We return at three and twelve months to check the record book and the pump.

How do you measure success?

Functionality at five years is our headline number, currently 87%. Behind it: down-time, repair cost and who paid, hygiene practice at follow-up visits, and whether the committee is still meeting.

What if a pump fails?

Our repair team responds within two weeks of a reported fault. Repairs triggered by a construction defect are free to the community; wear-and-tear repairs are met from the committee's fund, which is why the fund exists.

Do you work with district authorities?

Yes, on siting, permits, water quality testing and handover. District environmental health officers accompany our hygiene teams, which also keeps the work inside the public system after we leave.

Do you work outside Ghana?

Not at present. Our registration, teams and supply chain are Ghanaian, and we would rather deepen coverage here than open a second country.
